Patents you can play

Three of Danny Hillis's patents, read closely and then built as things you can actually operate. The mechanics come from the claims, not from the vibe of the drawings.

US 9,134,205 B2 · 2015
Dust Off

System and method for removing surface particles from an object

The airport puffer booth. An image sensor picks a spot on a person, a jet knocks particles loose, and capture jets herd them into a collector for analysis. Here the air jets are lasers. Tap the subject to ablate a patch, then hold the beam and steer the loose particles down into the intake before they float out of the chamber. Miss a flagged one and the subject walks uncleared.

US 7,857,767 B2 · 2010
Lumen

Lumen traveling device

A self propelling microrobot with wall gripping wheels, a sensor for a condition of interest, and an active portion that responds to it. You drive it. Steer through a branching vessel bed you can only see by lamplight, follow a sensor bearing that lies while you are far away, anchor against the flow, and burn out the plaque before the power cell runs down.

US 9,480,991 B2 · 2016
Separator

Radiofrequency particle separator

A radiofrequency field heats mineral grains until the water around them boils, and the vapour pocket floats the grain up to a skimmer. Frequency sets the skin depth, and separation works when the skin depth lands at about a tenth of the grain, so a big grain tunes low and a fine one tunes high. Ride the dial across the feed. Waste rock has no band of its own; it lifts whenever its size happens to match yours.

These are games, not implementations. All three take the arrangement of parts described in the claims and turn it into something you operate; none of them simulates the physics the patents actually rely on. Dust Off swaps the fluid jets for lasers on purpose, and Separator's frequencies are invented numbers that obey a real relation from the patent. All three are best on a laptop with a mouse or trackpad, and all three want a window about 1200 pixels wide or more.
